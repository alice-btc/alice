# Hyperinflation

## Questions covered

- What does hyperinflation describe?

## Short answer

Hyperinflation is an extremely rapid rise in prices accompanied by a loss of confidence in the currency. The threshold often associated with Cagan is inflation above 50% per month.

## Explanation

At such a pace, wages, savings and contracts become hard to interpret in terms of real purchasing power. People may try to spend very quickly or to use other units, which further changes the demand for money.

The phenomenon cannot be reduced to printing banknotes. It is understood within a context of financing, production, institutions and trust. Economic analyses debate the weight and the sequence of these mechanisms. A monthly indicator must not be confused with an annual change.

## Limits and confusions

Not every high inflation is a hyperinflation under this convention. The threshold is a classification tool, not a single natural law.

Interest in another currency during a crisis does not prove that this currency removes every risk. Bitcoin remains volatile; stablecoins add an issuer and a backing. Describing these options does not imply recommending a personal allocation or guaranteeing the protection of an income.
