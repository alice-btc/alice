# Bitcoin and energy — understanding costs and useful uses

## Questions covered

- What is the electricity consumed by Bitcoin used for?
- Can mining contribute to the energy transition?
- Does consuming electricity necessarily mean wasting it?

## Short answer

Mining spends electricity to take part in Bitcoin's proof of work. It can also provide concrete energy services, by buying production that has no local outlet, by reducing its demand when the grid needs it, by funding methane capture or by providing heating. The balance depends on the installation and on what would happen without it.

## Explanation

3 questions help in understanding a project. What energy does it consume? What service does it provide? What does it actually replace? Wind electricity that would have been curtailed and additional gas-fired production do not have the same climate effect.

In its survey published in 2025, Cambridge reports 52.4% so-called sustainable electricity among the miners surveyed, of which 42.6% renewable and 9.8% nuclear. The sample represents about 48% of the hashrate at the time of collection. This result describes a dated scope, not each mine nor an exhaustive measurement of the network.

*Orange is the New Green*, Alexandre Stachtchenko and Daniel Batten highlight these productive uses of energy. Their arguments are checked project by project, with data on consumption, curtailment and avoided emissions.

## Limits and confusions

Cheap energy is not necessarily lost energy. Mining does not store electricity to give it back later. Whether the Bitcoin currency is desirable is also a judgment about its uses; energy measurements do not settle that judgment on their own.
