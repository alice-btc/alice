# When mining can fund a reduction in methane emissions

## Questions covered

- How can mining with methane reduce emissions?
- What is the difference between vented gas and flared gas?
- Are there real projects or only models?

## Short answer

A generator can burn recovered methane and power miners. Their revenue can fund a collection system that would otherwise lack an outlet. The climate gain comes from the methane actually prevented from reaching the atmosphere, after accounting for combustion, leaks and the scenario without the project.

## Explanation

Methane can come from the decomposition of waste or be associated with hydrocarbon extraction. Venting releases the gas without burning it. Flaring burns it without directly using its energy. These situations do not provide the same point of comparison.

If the gas was being released, capturing it and burning it efficiently turns a large part of its methane into CO₂. This can strongly reduce the warming effect associated with the initial release. If a flare was already destroying it properly, the additional benefit is different. It depends notably on the comparative combustion of the equipment and on the use of the electricity produced. The IEA documents the emissions linked to inefficient or unlit flares; no single efficiency figure represents every site.

In an isolated landfill, selling gas or electricity can require costly infrastructure. Miners installed on site provide a customer for the generator. The EPA describes several other outlets for landfill gas, including heat and electricity. Mining's specific value is to add a sales option when those outlets are limited.

A real case was announced by Nodal Power in November 2023. The company describes a 280 kW mining pilot with Marathon in Utah, already in operation and powered by landfill gas. This operator statement documents a deployment; it does not by itself constitute an independent measurement of the net emissions avoided.

Rudd, Jones, Sechrest, Batten and Porter also published a model in the Journal of Cleaner Production in 2024, preceded by an SSRN preprint. Their Monte Carlo simulation examines the viability of pairing capture with mining. It shows revenue possibilities, with results sensitive to costs and to mining revenue. Its emissions scenario notably concerns a landfill that was previously unequipped and assumed to vent its gas. This is not a result transferable to a landfill already fitted with a flare.

## Limits and confusions

A credible assessment measures the gas collected, its methane content, leaks, downtime, destruction efficiency and the engine's emissions. It specifies the time horizon used to convert methane into CO₂ equivalent. The emissions from manufacturing and operating the equipment are part of the scope to examine.

The energy contained in the gas is not entirely converted into electricity. The generator's efficiency and its auxiliaries must be deducted. An estimate in kWh of gas cannot be presented directly as the same number of electrical kWh available to the ASICs.

The additional benefit depends on what would have been done without mining, including a capture obligation or another use. On an oil site, one must also examine whether this revenue helps extend production. An emissions reduction relative to a reference scenario does not mean removal of CO₂ already present in the air.

Daniel Batten is a co-author of the model and declares a stake in CH4 Capital, which is active in financing this type of operation. This interest should be known when evaluating the assumptions, without cancelling the physical mechanisms described.
