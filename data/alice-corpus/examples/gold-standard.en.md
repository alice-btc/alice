# The gold standard

## Questions covered

- How did a system tied to gold work?

## Short answer

The gold standard ties a monetary unit to a defined quantity of gold, with convertibility conditions. This system must be distinguished from directly holding coins or bars.

## Explanation

Users can circulate banknotes or deposits while relying on a promise of conversion. The link to gold reserves constrains issuance according to the rules of the regime. It also depends on the institutions that store the metal and honor that promise.

The arrangements changed across countries and periods. Who can convert, at what rate and under which restrictions are essential questions. Suspensions of convertibility show that the promised right and actual availability are not the same thing.

## Limits and confusions

A gold-backed system does not eliminate all credit or all counterparty risk. A convertible banknote is not identical to gold already under its holder's control.

The comparison with Bitcoin should preserve this distinction. The protocol's issuance rule is not a promise to convert each bitcoin into metal. Arguments about "monetary discipline" describe a mechanism and an economic reading, not a universal guarantee of stability.
