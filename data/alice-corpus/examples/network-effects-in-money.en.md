# Network effects in money

## Questions covered

- Why can the number of users matter?

## Short answer

A currency becomes more useful when more people can receive and use it. These network effects favor adoption, but also give strong inertia to currencies that are already established.

## Explanation

A user considers what they will be able to do after receiving a currency. Merchants, payment tools, exchange liquidity and accounting habits contribute to this usefulness. Network effects are therefore not limited to the number of accounts created.

For Bitcoin, they also include the infrastructure, developers, nodes and accumulated experience. Copying a piece of software's code does not automatically reproduce this set of relationships and trust. A technical innovation still has to be understood, used and maintained.

## Limits and confusions

A network effect guarantees neither universal adoption nor a permanent rise in price. Usage constraints, alternatives and custody risks can limit its development.

Actually active people, useful volumes and easily inflated indicators must be kept apart. The concept explains a possible dynamic; it does not provide a reliable formula for valuing Bitcoin or predicting its dominance. It remains useful for comparing technical adoption with monetary adoption.
