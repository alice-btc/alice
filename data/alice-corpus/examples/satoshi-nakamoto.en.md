# Satoshi Nakamoto

## Questions covered

- What is known about Satoshi Nakamoto?
- Which hypotheses exist about his identity?
- Does one need to know the creator to verify Bitcoin?
- Could Satoshi impose a change to Bitcoin?

## Short answer

Satoshi Nakamoto is the pseudonym under which the Bitcoin whitepaper was published in 2008, then its first software in 2009. The identity of the person or people behind this name remains unknown. Several hypotheses notably mention Adam Back, Hal Finney, Len Sassaman, Nick Szabo or Peter Todd; others consider a group. No identification has been demonstrated.

## Explanation

The whitepaper describes a payment system without a trusted third party in charge of keeping the ledger. Its announcement of 31 October 2008 is preserved in the archives of the Cryptography mailing list. These are accessible historical documents, unlike the civil identity of their author.

Research on Satoshi combines the history of cryptography, correspondence, language analyses and timelines. It can document connections without establishing an identity. The theory of a creation by the CIA also exists, but no evidence supports it in the sources verified for this note. It does not have the same level of documentation as the historical research on the people mentioned.

Knowing the creator is not necessary to examine the code or the rules enforced by a node. The creator has no special protocol power. Participants choose their software; a proposed change is not imposed on others merely because of its author. The software has evolved with contributions from many developers. Satoshi's identity could have a social or economic influence, without creating any particular authority in the protocol.

## Limits and confusions

- A similar style, a technical skill or a coincidence of timing is not enough to identify someone.
- A denial or a conviction expressed by an investigator does not settle the question on its own.
- Open code makes its examination possible. Audits and years of use do not guarantee the absence of flaws. Bitcoin Core publishes security advisories about identified vulnerabilities.
- This note turns neither an estimate of the bitcoins mined in the network's early days nor a mining analysis into a demonstrated fortune belonging to Satoshi.
