# Understanding inflation

## Questions covered

- What is inflation?
- Why does the same amount of money buy less?
- What is the difference between rising prices and an increase in money?

## Short answer

In everyday and statistical usage, inflation refers to a sustained and widespread rise in prices. Some authors also use the word for monetary expansion; that meaning needs to be specified.

## Explanation

Fictional example — if a basket costs 100 € and then 105 €, its price has risen by 5%. With 100 €, one can buy less of it. An inflation index tracks an average basket, which may differ from an individual's spending.

The quantity of money and prices are not the same measure. Their relationship depends notably on production, the demand for money, credit and circulation. Money creation can contribute to rising prices without producing an immediately proportional rise.

## Limits and confusions

A price increase for a single product does not establish general inflation. A statistical measure describes the evolution of a basket; by itself it does not settle the causes or the judgements about a monetary policy. Bitcoin's limited supply does not guarantee stable purchasing power.
