# When mining monetizes energy with no outlet

## Questions covered

- What does mining with surplus or stranded energy mean?
- Do miners consume energy that would otherwise have been wasted?
- Why not send that electricity to households?

## Short answer

Mining can buy electricity production that finds no consumer in the same place at the same time. This gives the producer revenue and can reduce curtailment. That surplus has to be identified on the site concerned; a low power price is not enough to prove it.

## Explanation

Curtailment consists of deliberately reducing the available production. A wind turbine can be throttled because the power line is saturated or because demand is low. In that case, the corresponding electricity is often not produced at all, rather than produced and then thrown away. NREL explained in 2022 that keeping production capacity above the demand of certain hours can also be a rational choice for the reliability of the system.

Stranded energy here refers to a resource that is hard to monetize because of isolation, a missing grid connection or insufficient demand. Mining can move its equipment close to the resource, use an Internet connection and sell its digital output without transporting the electricity to another country. It remains subject to roads, electrical equipment, cooling and maintenance.

Take a fictional example. A small power plant can supply 1 MW during certain hours, but the connected customers only ask for 600 kW. A mine could buy part of the 400 kW available. When local demand rises, that surplus shrinks. Control systems and a contract giving priority to the other customers then make it possible to reduce mining. The 400 kW are not a guaranteed year-round availability.

Gridless describes a model of this kind on African hydroelectric sites. The operator presents mining as a flexible buyer of unused electricity, with priority given to local needs. This documents a declared operating model; the quantities avoided, the prices and compliance with that priority are checked site by site. The review by Velický published by ACS in 2023 also examines the value of mining's mobility and flexibility for producers.

## Limits and confusions

Buying cheap electricity can also mean benefiting from a subsidized rate, a favorable contract or cheap fossil production. On a shared grid, this new demand can change production, prices and grid connection needs. There is therefore no general rule that miners never compete with households.

The right comparison includes the solutions available on site. A power line, a battery, pumped storage, industrial consumption or new customers can monetize the resource better. The IEA treats these solutions as complementary. Mining turns electricity into computation and heat; it does not make it possible to recover it electrically in the evening as a battery would.

A convincing project specifies the surplus hours, the meter used, how things work without the mine and the load reduction rules. It also shows that the expected revenue covers the equipment even with intermittent use.
