# How the rules evolve

## Questions covered

- Who can change Bitcoin?

## Short answer

Bitcoin evolves through proposals, software review and coordination between participants. No single leader can impose on all nodes rules that are incompatible with the ones they choose to enforce.

## Explanation

Developers propose and review code. Maintainers organize its integration into certain software. Users decide whether to use it; miners produce blocks that nodes verify. Economic actors also count in the acceptance of practices and chains.

Changing the code of a repository therefore does not instantly change the network. A proposal must be understood, implemented and then adopted through an appropriate mechanism. Divergences can lead to incompatible software or chains.

## Limits and confusions

The absence of a single authority does not mean the absence of influence, conflicts or dependencies. Review and coordination can protect stability while delaying improvements. Their slowness is not, on its own, proof of security.

Social governance must be distinguished from technical consensus. The rules say which blocks are valid for a node; the choice to change those rules belongs to a process broader than the miners' computation.
