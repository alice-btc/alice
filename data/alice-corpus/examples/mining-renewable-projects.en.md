# How mining can support a renewable project

## Questions covered

- Why would a renewable producer have mining on its site?
- Can mining help finance a power plant?
- What evidence separates a possibility from a realized benefit?

## Short answer

A mining installation can provide an additional outlet for a renewable producer. The revenue can support its operation or certain investments, notably when the grid connection or local demand is not yet sufficient. The outcome depends on the contract, the energy available and the economics of mining.

## Explanation

A producer incurs expenses before selling its electricity. If a plant is running but cannot yet export everything, a buyer installed on site can start paying for part of its production. It can then reduce its consumption when the grid or local customers absorb more. The value lies in this adaptation to the producer's situation.

The study by Lal and You published by ACS in 2023 examines use during the phase before the commercial grid connection of American wind and solar projects. Its results are economic simulations, not the accounts of mines actually installed. For its set of 32 Texas projects and its assumptions, it estimates a combined profit potential of 47 million dollars. This historical figure does not predict the return of a project launched today.

Gridless presents another case, that of small African renewable installations whose local demand develops gradually. The company describes a flexible buyer that monetizes the available electricity during this ramp-up. Its documentation makes it possible to understand the model and its declared achievements; establishing the causal effect on the construction of a plant also requires the project's contracts and financing.

Daniel Batten stresses these situations, which statistics based solely on a national electricity mix can misrepresent. His BEEST model adds information about sites and their energy sources. This work is a documentary lead for identifying operations; classifying a site within a low-carbon mix does not by itself establish that a new plant was built thanks to mining.

## Limits and confusions

3 outcomes must be distinguished. Buying renewable electricity is a purchase. Giving a producer revenue is an economic effect. Causing additional renewable capacity is an investment effect that requires a separate demonstration. The 3 can coincide, but they are not interchangeable.

The project's calculation must include the purchase of the machines, their useful life, the local grid connection, operating costs and the revenue per unit of computation. The bitcoin price, difficulty and transaction fees make this revenue vary. Downtime and the resale value of the hardware also change the balance. No cheap electricity contract guarantees a profit on its own.

An available renewable source can serve other customers, storage or a future grid connection. The most useful project is one whose contract accompanies this evolution. Mining revenue does not replace an analysis of local needs or an assessment of the other possible buyers.

The MDPI publication by Rudd and co-authors in 2023 notably proposes a research agenda on these interactions. Its status as a publication does not turn its possible benefits into effects already measured everywhere.
