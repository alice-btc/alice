# Hypotheses about Satoshi's identity

## Questions covered

- Why are Adam Back, Hal Finney, Len Sassaman, Nick Szabo or Peter Todd mentioned?
- Which arguments support or weaken these connections?
- Could Satoshi be a group?
- Is there any proof that the CIA created Bitcoin?
- How can historical facts, hypotheses and investigative conclusions be told apart?

## Short answer

Several investigations connect the pseudonym Satoshi Nakamoto to people from the cypherpunk milieu. They have not established his identity. A documented fact about a candidate can support a hypothesis without proving it. The overview below does not rank people and does not attribute the creation of Bitcoin to them.

## Explanation

### People mentioned and the limits of the connections

| Hypothesis | Documented arguments | Limits and objections |
|---|---|---|
| Adam Back | He created Hashcash, cited in the whitepaper. His court testimony reports emails with Satoshi from 20 August 2008, before Bitcoin was published. | This documents a technical precursor and a correspondence, not an identity. The emails show 2 parties; claiming he wrote to himself would add a hypothesis that requires its own evidence. [Back's testimony, 2023](https://bitcoindefense.org/assets/documents/First-Witness-Statement-of-Dr-Adam-Back.pdf). |
| Hal Finney | His work on RPOW and his participation in the first Bitcoin tests place him among the pioneers. He recounts receiving 10 BTC from Satoshi and corresponding with him to fix bugs. | His [personal account from 2013](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=155054.0) presents Satoshi as a different person. [Jameson Lopp's 2023 investigation](https://blog.lopp.net/hal-finney-was-not-satoshi-nakamoto/) matches a photographed race run by Finney against Satoshi messages from the same time. This weakens the hypothesis of Finney acting alone; participation in a group remains a separate, undemonstrated proposition. |
| Len Sassaman | Evan Hatch connects his work on PGP and remailers, his cypherpunk milieu and his death in July 2011 to Satoshi's withdrawal a few months earlier. | These connections are circumstantial. [Hatch's article](https://evanhatch.medium.com/len-sassaman-and-satoshi-e483c85c2b10) is the source of a hypothesis, not proof of identity. Meredith Patterson disputed the identification in a [direct interview in 2024](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/people-culture/len-sassaman-the-bitcoin-creator-satoshi-nakamoto/); her testimony must also be attributed, without letting it settle the matter on its own. |
| Nick Szabo | His [Bit Gold text from 2005](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bit-gold/) describes proof of work, timestamping and distributed property titles, before Bitcoin. | This proximity documents an intellectual antecedent, without proving that he wrote the Bitcoin software. Bit Gold and Bitcoin are not identical systems. Stylometric statistics or conclusions are not repeated here for lack of an exact, cross-checked reference. |
| Peter Todd | The 2024 HBO documentary interprets his reply to a Satoshi message in 2010 as a possible accidental account switch. | That is the director's interpretation. The original message was not cross-checked directly for this note. Todd explicitly denied it in an [email sent to CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2024/10/08/former-bitcoin-dev-peter-todd-denies-hes-satoshi-hours-before-hbo-documentary-airs). His jokes about being Satoshi do not constitute an admission. |

### A group behind the pseudonym

A pseudonym can be shared, and Bitcoin combines several areas of expertise. This makes a collaboration conceivable, without constituting proof that several people used the name Satoshi. The author of the project, its precursors, its reviewers and its first contributors must be distinguished.

The documentary *Finding Satoshi*, released on 22 April 2026, argues for a Finney–Sassaman collaboration. Its release is confirmed by the [producer](https://www.tooleyentertainment.com/documentary/finding-satoshi). The [direct interview with investigator Tyler Maroney](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2026-04-22-finding-satoshi-documentary-makes-case-hal-finney-len-sassaman-bitcoins-co-creators-398423) notably presents matches of skills and activity patterns, as well as objections. This note relies on that interview, not on a full viewing of the film. An investigative conclusion does not automatically become an established identification.

### The CIA theory

The documented fact is [Gavin Andresen's original announcement, on 27 April 2011](https://bitcointalk.org/?topic=6652.0), of a Bitcoin presentation planned at the CIA in June. An invitation to present an already public project does not demonstrate that the agency created it. The chronological proximity to Satoshi's withdrawal does not demonstrate causation either. No proof of a creation of Bitcoin by the CIA is established in the sources verified here.

### The evolution of the software since Satoshi

In a post dated 30 July 2023, Jameson Lopp estimated that **0.06% of the lines in the Bitcoin Core repository had remained unchanged since Satoshi**. This figure is attributed to its author and to that date; it is not a 2026 measurement. It represents neither the proportion of Satoshi's ideas still present nor his influence on the protocol. The post does not detail the method enough to reproduce this estimate on its own. [Official archive of the post](https://www.lopp.net/tweets/lopp/status/1685616945368805377/).

In his review published on 5 January 2025, Lopp also reports **276,000 lines added or removed in 2024** in Bitcoin Core. The published command adds up the insertions and deletions of commits; the same line can therefore be counted several times. This total describes development activity in 2024, not unique lines or a tally since 2009. No recalculation is presented here. [2024 review and published method](https://blog.lopp.net/bitcoin-2024-annual-review/).

## Limits and confusions

- Separate observation, interpretation and attribution. "Created Hashcash" is a documented fact; "therefore would have created Bitcoin" is an additional inference.
- A language analysis depends on the corpus, the choice of candidates and the method. A resemblance is not enough to prove an identity.
- A verifiable signature can demonstrate control of a key. By itself it establishes neither a civil identity nor that the person controlled that key at the time Bitcoin was created.
- Scenarios about motivations, anonymity, lost keys or the absence of spending remain hypothetical. They prove neither identity nor wealth.
- Patoshi is the name given to an analysis of early mining patterns. Its association with Satoshi and the current control of the keys are separate questions.
- A primary source attests to what a witness or an investigator says. It does not guarantee that all of their interpretations are correct.
- Knowing the creator's identity would give no special protocol power to impose changes. Verifying the code and the rules remains possible without knowing it; that possibility is not a guarantee of the absence of flaws.
